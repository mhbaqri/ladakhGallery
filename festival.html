Ladakhi Festivals

Celebrations, traditions and cultural performances



Suru Summer Festival

The Suru Summer Festival, initiated in the year 2025, features a lively mix of sports events, cultural programs, handicrafts, and traditional cuisine, continues to shine as a celebration of Kargil's unique identity and tourism potential.


Apricot Blossom Festival

Celebrated across different apricot cluster villages of Ladakh both in leh and Kargil during the peak blooming of apricot flowers, the Apricot Blossom Festival marks the joyful arrival of spring. Locally known as 'Chuli Mentok Stanmo' the festival showcases cultural performances, traditional practices, and local trade exhibitions.


Losar

Losar is the traditional New Year festival of Ladakh. It is one of the most important cultural and religious celebrations among the people of Ladakh. The word “Losar” comes from Tibetan words meaning “new year” (Lo = year, Sar = new).


Hemis Festival

The Hemis Festival is one of the most important and colourful Buddhist festivals celebrated in Ladakh. It is held at Hemis Monastery, one of the largest and most important monasteries in the region, located near Leh.


Dosmoche

Dosmoche is an important Buddhist festival celebrated in Ladakh. It is particularly associated with Leh, Likir and Nubra Valley. The festival is also known as the “Festival of the Scapegoat” because some of its rituals symbolically involve removing evil, misfortune and negative forces from the coming year.


Zanskar Festival

The Zanskar Festival is a colourful cultural festival celebrated in the Zanskar region of Ladakh. It showcases the unique culture, traditions, music, dance, handicrafts and lifestyle of the people of Zanskar.


Yuru Kabgyat

Yuru Kabgyat is an important Buddhist monastic festival held at Lamayuru Monastery in Ladakh Yuru Kabgyat is the annual masked-dance and religious festival of Lamayuru Monastery in Ladakh


Saka Dawa

Saka Dawa is one of the most sacred Buddhist observances celebrated in Ladakh and throughout Himalayan Buddhist communities. It is also called Saga Dawa or Saka Dawa Düchen.
